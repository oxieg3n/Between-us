# Child privacy and release requirements

Research date: 2026-11-16; updated for the owner's audience and onboarding decisions the same day. Status: proposed engineering and policy requirements for legal/child-safety review; not a legal opinion, compliance certification, or authorization to enroll families. Product intent: help preteens and teens learn communication and money-management skills, in as many US states and from as young an appropriate age as possible. **Guardian-managed setup together with the child/teen is required for every child/teen account, regardless of age.** This is a product invariant, not an optional setting; there is no 13+ self-signup bypass. Under-13 users are a first-class audience; a 13+ restriction is not the proposed compliance workaround. The previous ages 10–16 assumption is superseded: no exact minimum or maximum child age is approved. No state is yet approved. Synthetic development may exercise persistence, consent and report workflows; actual children's personal information, public enrollment, paid resources and production publication remain unauthorized.

The owner production directive authorizes this design work beyond the historical memory-only prototype. It does not make the older prototype's security claims production claims. Read alongside [boundaries](../security/BOUNDARIES.md), [production sequence](../security/PRODUCTION_SEQUENCE.md) and [release gates](../mission/PRODUCTION_RELEASE_GATES.md).

## Findings that affect the design

The April 22, 2025 COPPA amendments took effect June 23, 2025; the general compliance date was April 22, 2026, with specified safe-harbor-program exceptions. That deadline has passed. The amendments add biometric/government identifiers to covered information and strengthen disclosure consent, security and retention duties. They do not turn COPPA into a general under-18 law. [FTC rule publication](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/federal-register-notices/16-cfr-part-312-coppa-final-rule-amendments), [Federal Register final rule and dates](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm).

COPPA defines children as under 13. Child-directed services and general-audience services with actual knowledge require analysis; marketing as a family app or offering an age selector does not establish an exemption. Collection includes prompting submission and passive tracking. Treat proposed under-13 enrollment as covered pending counsel's assessment. Teen enrollment needs a separate state-law analysis. [Current 16 CFR Part 312](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312).

Direct notice must precede covered collection and explain the actual collection/use/disclosure and consent process. The online notice needs operator contacts, practices and parental rights. A material practice change can require fresh consent; a privacy-policy link alone is insufficient. [16 CFR 312.4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.4).

VPC must be reasonably calculated to establish that the person consenting is the parent. Separate consent is required for non-integral third-party disclosures; participation cannot be conditioned on that optional consent. Limited exceptions exist, including narrow contact-to-obtain-consent uses. Email/text “plus” methods are restricted to non-disclosure cases. Do not assume a service that enables identifiable messages qualifies for those methods. [16 CFR 312.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.5).

Parents must be able to review covered information, request deletion, and refuse further collection/use; identity checks protect those requests. This conflicts with an unconditional promise that a guardian can never obtain an under-13 child's writing. [FTC COPPA guide, parental access and consent sections](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions). The journal decision below is unresolved; it is not permission to add a guardian read endpoint.

Operators need a written security program, a designated coordinator, at least annual risk assessment and safeguards evaluation, and written security assurances from relevant service providers/third parties. [16 CFR 312.8](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.8). Retention must serve specific purposes, end when reasonably unnecessary, and never be indefinite; the written schedule belongs in the online notice. [16 CFR 312.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.10).

The eCFR part was initially accessible (displayed current through 2026-11-12); some subsequent section fetches were access-limited. The published final rule and FTC guidance supplied corroboration. Recheck current rule text and intervening amendments at legal approval.

## Platform release checklist

Apple Guidelines 1.2 require safeguards for user-generated content, including filtering, reporting/timely response, blocking and published contact information. Kids Category restrictions and parental gates need separate evaluation; a parental gate is not VPC. Decide category/age rating and how safety resources remain accessible before submission. No third-party advertising/analytics is proposed. [Apple App Review Guidelines, 1.2, 1.3 and 5.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

Apple requires account-deletion initiation inside account-creating apps, including associated data except legally required retention; mere deactivation is insufficient. Ordinary apps should not require a call/email support flow to delete. Reauthentication may protect the action without obstructing it. [Apple account deletion guidance](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion).

Google Families requires truthful audience/Data safety declarations and appropriate SDK/data handling where children are targeted. Social features need safety reminders, adult management and adult action before children exchange personal information. This adult-action requirement does not itself prove guardianship or satisfy COPPA VPC. No stranger/anonymous chat is proposed. [Google Play Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335).

Google requires an in-app deletion path and a functioning external web deletion-request resource for account-creating apps. Publish actual exceptions/timeframes in the privacy policy and answer the Data deletion form accurately. [Google account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111).

The separate Google Child Safety Standards cover Social/Dating categories and anonymous/random chat apps. If applicable, they require published CSAE standards, in-app feedback, action on CSAM, applicable-law compliance and a designated child-safety contact. Store classification and the responsible operator must be decided; Families review alone is insufficient. [Google Child Safety Standards guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14747720?hl=en).

## Onboarding and authority requirements

Proposed states: `unclassified -> guardianEnrollmentPending -> noticePending -> consentPending -> relationshipPending -> childTeenAcceptancePending -> eligible`; independent flags for age assurance, teen grants, social-feature adult action and verified contacts. Every child/teen account needs authenticated guardian enrollment intent and verified setup authority; a teen's own consent or birthday never substitutes for this product requirement. Denial states include `unsupported`, `withdrawn`, `disputed`, `suspended`, `deleting`, `deleted`. No state transition itself grants journal access or permission to contact a child.

1. Offer readable safety resources without login, consent or guardian notification. Do not collect journal/message/profile content to determine eligibility. No tracking, crash payloads with content, advertising identifiers, contact-book upload or third-party SDK initialization before the applicable gate.
2. Require guardian-managed setup **with** the child/teen for all child/teen accounts, including ages 13 and above. The server must bind a scoped, expiring, single-use enrollment intent to the authenticated verified guardian, intended child and approved jurisdiction/age policy; reject public child/teen self-signup, replay, unrelated guardian substitution and client-supplied bypass flags. Under-13 support is a primary flow with age-appropriate assistance. Obtain only the neutral age signal and coarse guardian-declared state needed under the approved method; no GPS, precise address or device fingerprinting to determine state. Prefer a derived age band/next transition date over retaining full birth date when sufficient. Do not suggest a bypass age; reject unsupported users rather than silently recategorizing them. An under-13 child must not provide an ordinary email/password account or content before lawful authorization. A guardian declaration routes eligibility review; it does not itself prove age, VPC or legal authority.
3. If relying on an exception to contact a parent for consent, document exactly permitted fields, one purpose, pending expiry and deletion. Proposed pending window: 7 days, no marketing, no feature access. A refusal deletes pending child/contact data promptly. “Consent pending” is not a limited trial with real writing.
4. Give the guardian direct notice identifying the operator, child-data categories, purposes, recipients/integral messaging disclosures, retention, rights, required versus optional choices and the consent method. Deliver the exact approved notice before verification/grant; record version/hash, recipient reference, delivery result and time. Failed delivery cannot count as notice.
5. Select a counsel-approved VPC method appropriate to actual disclosures; no checkbox, typed name, adult PIN, email link or role selector alone is accepted as VPC. A permitted method is not automatically adequate in this context. Do not collect government ID/biometrics in the app as a shortcut. Prefer narrowly scoped verifier attestations with contractual controls; raw evidence handling remains unapproved.
6. Verify the claimed guardian relationship/authority separately. Authenticated email and completed VPC do not prove custody. Require a reviewed process for multiple guardians, foster care, restrictions and conflicting claims without asking a child to upload court documents.
7. Explain the experience to the child/teen and require their own age-appropriate acceptance/assent. Create a distinct child/teen authentication identity with their own approved credentials; guardian approval must not become ordinary guardian impersonation or reuse of the adult's session as the child. Obtain the teen's own grants where legally required, independently of guardian product approval and any under-13 VPC. Neither decision replaces the other. Denied optional processing must leave the core permitted service available. Do not sell data, personalize ads, or reuse writing/model inputs for training.
8. Enable only the approved capabilities. Child contact approval, invite acceptance, independent verification, current consent, block state and social-feature adult action remain separate checks at review/send/delivery/subscription. Check revocation after offline work and between review/send. No automatically copied journal text.
9. Reassess on birthday, declared relocation, purpose/vendor change and guardian change. Turning 13 does not erase prior records, transfer keys to a guardian, or authorize optional processing; request the applicable teen decisions. Define every approved age-band transition and the eventual adult transition rather than assuming a fixed age-16 exit.

The minimum-age decision must include usability/readability evidence for the youngest proposed group, understanding of recipient/review/send, optionality and money amounts, ability to recover from mistakes, accessibility, and comprehension of journal loss/report visibility. Test the developmental design with appropriate review and authorization before involving real children; current work remains synthetic. Provide plain language and optional accessible explanations without pressuring disclosure. Education about money must remain an age-appropriate household planning tool, with no custody, transfer, investment recommendation or reward tied to private writing.

Approve a credential policy per age group before enabling accounts: required guardian bootstrap together with the child/teen and device pairing, distinct child/teen authentication, their own age-appropriate sign-in/unlock and acceptance, shared-device separation, rate limiting, lost-device/recovery and unsafe-guardian scenarios. Do not require younger children to control personal email/phone, memorize adult-grade credentials, or share a guardian's credential. A simplified child credential is not guardian authentication or VPC. Guardian-assisted account setup/recovery must never grant the guardian journal keys, private drafts, or an ordinary impersonation session. Guardian-managed enrollment does not gate safety information or seeking help from another safe adult; those independent paths remain available without guardian approval.

Minimum consent receipt: random receipt ID; subject pseudonymous ID; actor ID; authority type and verification reference; jurisdiction/age policy version; purpose IDs; grant/deny choices; notice version/hash/delivery; VPC method/version/result/attestation reference; granted/withdrawn timestamps; superseded receipt; expiry/recheck reason; reviewing operator when applicable. Keep no journal text, raw identification, payment-card number or speculative family-risk label in this receipt. Server derives actor/subject authority; clients cannot self-assert verified flags. Minimize IP/device logs and document any approved use separately.

## Withdrawal, rights, guardian changes and disputes

- Provide accessible grant history and withdrawal for each optional purpose; make withdrawal as easy as grant. Core-consent withdrawal immediately prevents new covered collection, sending, queued delivery, subscriptions, scheduled notifications and token use as appropriate; separate deletion follows. Safety resources remain available. Do not wait for the deletion batch to revoke authority.
- Accept authenticated own-account export/deletion and a separate verified parental-rights request. Do not disclose that a child/account exists to an unverified claimant. Use proportionate verification and a secure response channel; do not require additional sensitive content to locate an account.
- Export only the requester's authorized server-held records, with recipient/third-party data redaction reviewed. Guardian family administration cannot export another author's journal, drafts or confidential report. A parental statutory request affecting those categories enters legal review under the journal gate rather than receiving an automatic blanket denial or a master-key bypass.
- Deletion must show scope, timing, permitted exceptions and device limitations before confirmation. Provide in-app account deletion and a public web request path at release; neither requires an unsafe guardian's approval to seek help. Stop invitations and re-registration resurrection; erase/revoke sessions, push tokens, pending consent records and vendor copies; propagate deletion tombstones through backup restoration. Record completion without retaining the deleted payload.
- A new guardian never inherits an old grant, journal key, private draft or report access. Verify authority, suspend disputed administrative/contact changes, review safe communications and issue new scoped grants where necessary. Revoke superseded authority and sessions; do not automatically notify an alleged unsafe adult or expose the reporting child's safe contact.
- A trained restricted reviewer, independent of the reported guardian, handles disputes and appeals. Define authority evidence, response deadlines, confidentiality limits, legal escalation and safe interim access. No automated custody decision. Preserve only specifically authorized evidence under a documented, time-bounded legal hold, reviewed regularly.

## Unresolved journal / parental-rights gate

Target architecture: device-only, author-owned SQLCipher journal; no cloud journal synchronization, no journal upload to report/AI/telemetry, and no guardian/operator journal keys or administrator read/export endpoint. Encryption and key-custody claims require independent implementation review. Author-initiated local export is separate from server account export. A shared or compromised unlocked device can still expose writing.

There is no proposed key escrow or silent account-recovery access. Lost device, app removal, lost key or key invalidation can mean permanent journal loss; account recovery must explain that before any destructive step. Disable journal OS/cloud backup until a reviewed encrypted backup and key design is approved; do not assume a platform default excludes it. Server deletion cannot truthfully guarantee erasure on an unreachable device or copies a recipient/user exported.

Counsel must decide whether and how COPPA collection/access/deletion obligations apply to this local functionality and the complete service. The FTC FAQ gives a narrower example: merely interacting with a device-stored photo that is never transmitted is not collection. That example is relevant but does not establish the answer for authored journals, account-linked metadata, SDKs, exports or backups in this service. [FTC FAQ, device-stored-photo example](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions).

Local storage is not asserted to be outside COPPA. An ordinary family-admin denial does not resolve a legally valid parent request; likewise, a parent request does not justify engineering an undisclosed key bypass. Required written decision: covered data/categories, operator capability, lawful review/deletion mechanism, child safety/custody handling and accurate child/guardian notice. Resolving this for younger children is part of the intended product, not a reason to silently replace it with a 13+ service. Until resolved, real-data journal activation stays closed; if the requirements cannot coexist, return the specific conflict to the owner for a reviewed product decision. Do not weaken the boundary silently or merely exclude journal bytes from an inventory.
